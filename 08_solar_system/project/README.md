# Solar-system orrery — from JPL's ephemeris to a live sky

## The problem

Where is every planet, right now — and how do you *know* you got it right? NASA/JPL
publishes the **DE440** planetary ephemeris (the same tables that navigate spacecraft). We
load it, compute each planet's **heliocentric, J2000-ecliptic** position at any instant, and
build a **live orrery** you can scrub through time. The numbers are validated against an
independent NASA service (**JPL Horizons**) and against the **physics** (Kepler's third law).

## Physics / geometry scaffold

The ephemeris gives positions in the **ICRF** (an equatorial frame). The ecliptic — the
plane of Earth's orbit — is tilted from the equator by the obliquity **ε ≈ 23.439°**. To get
a flat, top-down orrery we rotate each vector about the x-axis by ε:

> **r_ecliptic = Rₓ(ε) · r_equatorial**,  ε = 84381.448″ (IAU76, J2000)

Subtract the Sun (not the solar-system barycenter) for heliocentric vectors, and use
**geometric** positions (no light-time) so they match Horizons' geometric vectors exactly.

The truth oracle is **Kepler's third law**: in solar units, **P²(yr) = a³(AU)**. We recover
each planet's period (longitude advancing by 2π) and semi-major axis ((r_min+r_max)/2) from
the ephemeris and check the law holds across all eight planets.

> Headline: Earth ≈ 1.000 AU / 365.3 d; Jupiter ≈ 5.20 AU / 11.86 yr; P²/a³ = 1.000 ± 0.001
> for all eight planets — recovered straight from de440s.

## External answer key (and an honest caveat)

Positions are checked against **JPL Horizons** (`astroquery.jplhorizons`). But Skyfield
(`de440s`) and Horizons derive from the **same JPL ephemeris family** — so this validates our
*usage* (frame rotation, heliocentric subtraction, geometric vs light-time), **not the
ephemeris model itself**. The genuinely independent check is the **physics**: Kepler's third
law recovered from the positions. (Cf. demo 02, where two *unrelated* methods agree.)

## Data provenance

`data/kernels/de440s.bsp` is JPL's **DE440** short ephemeris (public domain), fetched directly
from **NASA NAIF** (`naif.jpl.nasa.gov/.../de440s.bsp`). `data/reference/horizons_positions.csv`
is geometric heliocentric-ecliptic vectors from **JPL Horizons**. Both are regenerated by
`scripts/fetch_kernel.py`. Nothing is fabricated.
